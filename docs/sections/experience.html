PhD Candidate

Doctor of Philosophy · Theoretical and Mathematical Physics
ICB Dijon, Université de Bourgogne, France

The research focuses on fundamental questions in quantum control: how cheap, fast, and robust can a quantum operation be made? Working with both analytical tools, particularly the Pontryagin Maximum Principle, and numerical methods such as GRAPE, the work tackles thermodynamic costs, work fluctuations, and timing constraints in quantum gate design. A second thread extends this to open quantum systems, where noise and dissipation from the environment make the problem harder and the answers more relevant.

MS Thesis Researcher

BS-MS Integrated · Physics
IISER Berhampur, India

Developed reinforcement learning based optimal control protocols for quantum batteries, targeting maximum ergotropy in spin-1/2 chain systems. Starting from a single-qubit system validated against known results, the work extended to multi-qubit configurations (2 to 5 qubits) under both ordered and disordered dynamics, demonstrating that RL-guided control outperforms greedy protocols for many-body quantum battery charging.
