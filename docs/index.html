Past & Present

How I Got Here

I began with a BS-MS in Physics at IISER Berhampur, where I built my foundation in theoretical physics and developed a lasting interest in quantum systems and mathematical structure.

During my master's thesis, I worked under Victor Mukherjee and Paolo Andrea Erdman, where I explored quantum battery problems and learned to think more seriously about control, optimization, and physically meaningful performance measures.

I now work at ICB Dijon as a PhD candidate under Dominique Sugny and Camille, with a current focus on optimal control, robust control strategies, Pontryagin-based formulations, and the numerical methods needed to make these ideas useful in realistic quantum settings.

Future Directions

Where I Want To Go

I want to keep pushing deeper into robust and numerically efficient quantum control, but I am equally interested in the broader space around it: open quantum systems, simulation-heavy workflows, machine-learning-assisted control, and theory that can survive contact with implementation.

I am also interested in problems where abstract physics can be translated into methods, software, or workflows that are genuinely useful beyond a single paper. This includes learning from adjacent areas and building the technical range needed to move between theory, numerics, and practical research tools.

This section is still evolving, but the long-term direction is clear: develop stronger control methods, stronger computational tools, and stronger intuition for how deep theory becomes practical value.

Mindset

My Philosophy

Where My Curiosity Begins

What has stayed with me across different problems is a certain kind of curiosity: the wish to understand why something works before deciding how to work with it.

That is part of what draws me to quantum control. I like problems where physical intuition, mathematical structure, and constructive method have to stay in conversation with each other. The appeal, for me, is not only in solving a specific problem, but in understanding the shape of it well enough to know what can be controlled, what can be optimized, and what kind of solution is actually meaningful.

That same pull is also why I keep finding myself interested in nearby areas such as optimization, learning, algorithms, and error correction. They may look different on the surface, but they often circle around related questions of structure, reliability, and method.

Starting from Structure

Before reaching for a technique, I usually want to understand the structure of the problem itself: what is constrained, what is preserved, and what kinds of solutions are even possible. That habit comes from physics, but it has shaped how I think more generally. A method chosen too early can work in a limited setting and still miss the deeper logic of the problem.

Why Learning Interests Me

What interests me in machine learning is not only the use of algorithms, but the larger question underneath them. How does a system, after being exposed to enough examples, begin to extract structure and respond well to situations it has not seen before? What kind of representation is being formed, and what is actually being simplified or compressed? Questions like these are part of why learning interests me beyond application alone.

Following Ideas Across Fields

I often find myself drawn to ideas that appear again in different settings. Sometimes the same pattern returns in another language: a variational point of view, a notion of robustness, a constrained optimization picture, or a way of thinking about stability and error. When that happens, I usually take it seriously, because it often points to something deeper underneath.

💻

Programming and Clarity

I tend to trust an idea more after I have tried to build it. Programming has a way of exposing vague understanding very quickly. It forces assumptions into the open and makes it harder to hide behind elegant language. For me, that is one of its real intellectual values. It is not separate from thinking through a problem, but part of how I test whether an idea is precise enough to hold.

Moving across ideas, for me, is not a way of leaving a field behind, but a way of seeing it more clearly.

Selected Projects

01

Quantum Battery Charging via Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement Learning Quantum Batteries Optimal Control Soft Actor-Critic Disordered Systems

This project studies how to design efficient charging protocols for quantum batteries, where the goal is not only to store energy but to maximize the useful work that can later be extracted from the system. In this setting, the figure of merit is ergotropy, and the control problem is defined over spin-chain quantum batteries driven by an external modulation.

The central idea was to reformulate battery charging as an optimal-control problem and solve it using reinforcement learning, specifically the Soft Actor-Critic framework. The study considered spin-chain systems with 2-5 qubits, trained control policies over fixed time intervals, and compared the learned protocols against greedy charging strategies. The framework was then extended to disordered systems to test whether reinforcement-learning-based control remains effective when the underlying dynamics become less structured.

The results show that learned protocols can provide strong charging performance for few-body quantum batteries and can outperform simpler greedy approaches in relevant regimes. Beyond the specific battery model, the project also serves as an example of how machine learning can be used as a practical tool for quantum control when the search space becomes too complicated for direct intuition-driven protocol design.
