Mindset

My Philosophy

I am interested in places where understanding becomes method.

I am most drawn to problems that sit at the boundary between physical insight and constructive method. Quantum control is one such space, but not the only one. Questions in optimization, learning, algorithms, and error correction often attract me for the same reason: they ask how structure can be understood well enough to be shaped, improved, or made reliable.

My interest in machine learning is not limited to using it as a convenient tool. I am equally interested in the broader idea of learning itself: how systems extract pattern, how useful representations emerge, and how flexible methods can be built without losing interpretability.

Although my work is grounded in a specific area, I do not like treating fields as closed boxes. I value moving across neighboring ideas when that movement leads to deeper intuition, better methods, or a wider sense of what is possible.

🧠

Physical Insight

I care about understanding the system before trusting the method. For me, theory is not separate from computation; it defines what is meaningful, what is controllable, and what is worth optimizing.

🛠️

Constructive Method

I am not satisfied by elegant ideas alone. I am most interested when an idea can be turned into an algorithm, a workflow, or a usable numerical strategy that survives practical constraints.

🌐

Exploratory Range

Even though my current work is centered on quantum control, I naturally keep returning to adjacent areas such as optimization, machine learning, quantum algorithms, and error correction, because they often illuminate similar questions from different directions.
