Energy Shortcut of N-Level Quantum Protocols by Optimal Control

PRL ⏳
C. L. Latune, M. B. Puthuveedu Shebeek, D. Sugny, S. Guérin · arXiv:2503.20130 · 2025
Optimal Control Energy Efficiency Shortcut to Adiabaticity GRAPE N-level Systems

This work introduces QOSTE (Quantum-Optimal-Shortcut-To-Energetics), a framework for designing quantum control protocols that achieve the same state transformations as Shortcut-To-Adiabaticity methods but at the lowest possible energy cost. The central result is that the minimal energy cost is governed by the length of a geodesic in the rotating frame defined by the original protocol, giving the method a clean geometric interpretation.

The framework is developed for general N-level quantum systems using geometric and optimal control techniques. For extended control durations, energy cost ratios are shown to scale quadratically with time. The approach was tested against standard protocols including Landau-Zener and STIRAP, demonstrating substantial energy reductions in both cases.

Optimization further reveals a quantifiable trade-off between energy efficiency and robustness. QOSTE designs are shown to outperform conventional methods on both fronts simultaneously, establishing a practical path toward energy-aware quantum gate design.

02

Quantum Battery Charging via Reinforcement Learning

M. B. Puthuveedu Shebeek, V. Mukherjee, P. A. Erdman · MS Thesis · 2024
Reinforcement Learning Quantum Batteries Optimal Control Soft Actor-Critic Disordered Systems

This project studies how to design efficient charging protocols for quantum batteries, where the goal is to maximize the useful work extractable from the system. The figure of merit is ergotropy, and the control problem is defined over spin-chain quantum batteries driven by an external modulation.

Battery charging was reformulated as an optimal-control problem and solved using the Soft Actor-Critic framework. The study covered spin-chain systems with 2 to 5 qubits, comparing learned protocols against greedy strategies, and extended to disordered systems to test robustness under less structured dynamics.

Results show that learned protocols outperform greedy approaches in relevant regimes and demonstrate how reinforcement learning can serve as a practical tool for quantum control when direct intuition-driven design becomes intractable.

JED 2025

Robust and Energy-Efficient Controls for a Two-Level System

Mohammed Bilal, C. L. Latune, D. Sugny · ICB Dijon
Poster Optimal Control Robust Control Energy Efficiency PMP

We consider a two-level quantum system governed by a Landau-Zener Hamiltonian, with the goal of designing control protocols that transfer the system between states within a fixed time. The objectives are to minimize the energy cost of the control field while maintaining high fidelity, and to ensure robustness against realistic parameter uncertainties such as calibration errors in the control amplitude.

The optimal energy-minimizing solution is derived analytically using the Pontryagin Maximum Principle. Robustness is incorporated through a gradient-ascent algorithm that optimizes an ensemble-averaged figure of merit over uncertain system parameters, yielding a trade-off curve between energy cost and robustness. The controls outperform Shortcut-to-Adiabaticity on both metrics simultaneously.

This poster presents work now submitted to PRL. See arXiv:2503.20130 for the full paper.

Cargese 2026

Universally Robust Control for a Qubit under an Unknown Perturbation

Mohammed Bilal P. S., C. L. Latune, D. Sugny · ICB Dijon · Cargese Summer School
Poster Quantum Control Robust Control Qubit Gate Synthesis

This poster studies how to design quantum controls that remain high-fidelity when the Hamiltonian contains an unknown static perturbation. The framework treats both state-to-state transfer and gate synthesis for a qubit driven by Pauli-basis controls.

Robustness is imposed through perturbative cancellation conditions on the Dyson expansion. The state-transfer examples retain near-unity averaged fidelity over the perturbation interval, and the same idea is extended to a robust NOT gate.

Presented at Cargese Summer School "Quantum Technologies for Computation and Communication", 8-20 June 2026, IESC Cargese.
